A power source that sits inert in storage and switches on when water arrives.

WEG is a patented galvanic cell that uses water as its electrolyte. Dry, it does nothing. When water reaches it, it starts producing electricity. For flood and leak sensing, the event you want to detect is the thing that powers the sensor.

The problem

Flood sensors wait years for one event

  • Batteries run down while nothing happens, through standby current and self-discharge.
  • Checks and replacements are a recurring field cost across thousands of units, and they get skipped.
  • A sensor that is flat on the day of the flood is a failed product, an insurance claim and a lost customer.
  • Lithium primary cells add cost, shipping rules and end-of-life waste to low-cost devices.

How WEG works

Water is the electrolyte

Each cell pairs an aluminium anode with a copper cathode. Water, plain or salty, closes the circuit, and the aluminium is slowly consumed to produce current.

  • Dry storage: no electrolyte, so no reaction before deployment.
  • Wet: output starts within seconds and continues while water is present.
  • Materials: aluminium and copper. No lithium, no acids, no heavy metals.
  • Scales by cells: cells in series raise voltage; more area raises current.
Dry

The cell is inert. Nothing is consumed.

Water arrives

Water becomes the electrolyte between aluminium and copper.

Power

A harvesting PMIC charges a small storage capacitor.

Alert

The microcontroller wakes and reports the event. Power-on is the alarm.

What we can say today

A microwatt source, built for long life

~3 Vfrom six 10 × 2 cm cells in plain water, as measured in the patent
µA classcontinuous current: designed for wake-on-water and low duty cycles, not high power
Inert dryno electrolyte in the cell until water arrives
Al / Cucommon, low-cost, non-toxic materials; plain or salt water

Independent measurements with a university partner are under way, covering output over time in different waters. ESP32-S3 alarm firmware that sends a "WATER DETECTED" push notification on power-up is ready for pilot testing. We do not claim high power or unlimited life: the aluminium anode is consumed while wet, and life depends on cell size and load.
